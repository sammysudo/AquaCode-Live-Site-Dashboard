Curiosity corner

What do these water words mean?

Every reading is a clue. Tap a topic to uncover what the science is measuring and what it cannot tell us.

pHWater balance

pH is a scale from 0 to 14 that describes how acidic or basic a liquid is. Seven is neutral; lower numbers are more acidic, and higher numbers are more basic.

Imagine: Lemon juice is acidic. Soapy water is basic. Our dashboard uses 6.5–8.5 as its learning guide.

TDS & ppmDissolved solids

Total dissolved solids (TDS) estimates substances dissolved in water, such as minerals and salts. ppm means “parts per million”—a way of describing a very small amount.

Remember: TDS is not a germ detector. A low TDS number does not prove that water is safe to drink.

TurbidityCloudiness

Turbidity describes how much tiny suspended material makes water look cloudy. It is often measured in NTU (nephelometric turbidity units).

Look closely: Clear-looking water can still contain germs or chemicals that eyes cannot see.

TemperatureHow warm it is

Temperature tells us how warm or cool the sample is, in degrees Celsius (°C). It can also affect how some sensors behave.

Try this: Measure the same water at different temperatures and see whether the other readings change.

How the result is decided

The SAFE / NOT SAFE rule

SAFE: pH is 6.5–8.5 and TDS after cleaning is 500 ppm or less. If either check is outside its limit, the result is NOT SAFE.

Important: These checks do not test for every germ or chemical. A “good” result is not proof that water is safe to drink. Always ask a trusted adult and follow local water-safety guidance.
